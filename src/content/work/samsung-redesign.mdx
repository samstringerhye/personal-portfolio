<p class="cs-intro">I set the visual direction for Samsung.com's global navigation, the menu every shopper in 12+ markets uses to find a product. I was a Senior Designer on a twelve-designer, six-month redesign, and I also built page layouts and contributed components to the design system. The GNB was the hardest problem and took most of my time.</p>

<ProseGroup>

## The Problem

Samsung.com's navigation had accumulated complexity from years of market-by-market additions. Different product catalogs meant different category hierarchies. Different languages meant different string lengths and overflow behaviors. Right-to-left scripts flipped the entire spatial logic. Every market had its own workarounds. The challenge was designing a navigation strict enough to read as one brand globally, but flexible enough that no market needed to break it.

</ProseGroup>

  ## Global Navigation

  The navigation had too many levels, and cutting depth couldn't mean hiding products. My visual direction put a product image beside every category, from the desktop menu down to a mobile drill-in, so a shopper spots the Galaxy Z Flip or the 75-inch TV before reading its label. I prototyped variations and stress-tested each against right-to-left scripts, deep catalogs, and long item lists, aiming for one navigation no market needed to fork.

## Outcomes

Over six months, I delivered the global navigation's visual direction, prototypes stress-tested against right-to-left scripts and deep catalogs, layouts for four core page types (homepage, product detail, category, and family), and components for the shared design system.

</ProseGroup>

<ProseGroup>

## What I Learned

The GNB problem was information architecture before it was visual design. Designing for the hardest case first forced decisions about hierarchy, truncation, and overflow that a single-locale design would never surface. Starting with the most complex market's catalog depth and script direction as the baseline meant every edge case was handled by default. At global scale, the job is writing constraints regional teams can work within.

</ProseGroup>
