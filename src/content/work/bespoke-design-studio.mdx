## The Problem

A Bespoke fridge means picking a model, then a size, then colors for four door panels. Show it all at once and people bail, as the early prototypes confirmed. Show too little and they don't understand their options. In a store, a salesperson sets the order. Online, the interface has to.

</ProseGroup>

## Wireframes

Multiple rounds of wireframing explored how to sequence the configuration steps. The core tension: each decision depended on the previous one (you can't pick panel colors until you've chosen a model), but users needed to understand the full scope before committing to step one.

## The Progress Indicator

The sequencing call needed a visible spine. I designed a stepped progress indicator that showed the scope of remaining decisions upfront, so users could see they were three steps from done rather than wondering if the choices would ever end.

</ProseGroup>

## Outcomes

The refrigerator configurator's add-to-cart rate was more than 6 times that of standard product pages, and the kitchen configurator's more than 10 times, with order rates up more than 50% on both. By mid-2023 the tool was running a 73% customization rate and a 66% continuation rate. In the 12 months to May 2024, the kitchen simulator beat its add-to-cart target by 36 points, and nearly half of its buyers bought more than one appliance.

The configurator kept growing after launch. It expanded from refrigerators to laundry in August 2022, then to a full kitchen simulator in April 2024 that carried 36 models, 18 of them new. An April 2025 update added 25 SKUs, and a separate build ran on Conn's and P.C. Richard's sites.

</ProseGroup>

<ProseGroup>

## What I Learned

The hardest part of this project was sequencing. Progressive disclosure is a series of trade-offs: every decision you defer to a later step is a decision the user might not reach. What worked was turning an open-ended process into a finite one. Once users could see exactly how many decisions remained, they stopped wondering if the choices would ever end and started moving through them.

</ProseGroup>
