# Reference sources for case study writing

Notes and verdicts only. No article text is reproduced here. Verdicts: useful, marginal, off-topic.

## Read in full

- **Van Schneider, "A visual guide to writing portfolio case studies"** (useful). Seven steps: problem, role, solution, how it solves the problem, challenges, impact, learnings. Captions carry the story. Don't invent numbers.
- **Van Schneider, blog post on writing project case studies** (useful). Draft copy before layout. Caption every image in one or two sentences. Cover client, goal, process decisions, outcome, and role. Credit your role explicitly.
- **Behance, "Building the Perfect Case Study"** (useful). Opening, problem, process, solution, reflection. Explain why decisions were made, not only what they produced. End with what you would change. Title is topic-based.
- **TylerD&I, "An Introduction to Case Studies for Designers"** (useful). Five-part spine: intro, reframe the problem, approach, deliverables, conclusion. Specific claims beat vague ones. Edit for structure, and read the piece aloud.
- **Semplice, "How to write case studies"** (useful). Put the type of work in the title. Set the goal in one sentence. Ask "why" until each answer is in the text. Keep the read to about three minutes. Admit when a result wasn't a win.
- **Toptal, "Dissecting case study portfolios"** (useful). Sequence: client, problem, role and constraints, solution, process, results, learnings, conclusion. Anonymize NDA work and say what was withheld. Metrics must be explainable to a non-expert. Keep three to five studies.
- **UXfolio, UX case study template** (useful, commercial caveat: the publisher sells a builder). Outcome-led titles. Overview covers role, tools, team, methods, and problem. Each method answers why, what it found, and how it changed the next step. Impact gets its own section. Length guidance conflicts within the article (600 vs 800 to 1,500 words).
- **Ellen Covey, "The Beakery"** (useful as an exemplar). Metadata block, a one-line goal, a short origin story, specific counts, results tied back to the goal. Gap to avoid: missing business outcomes.
- **Mayerr, "How to make a case study"** (useful). Choose projects that show the next job. Open strongly for the skimmer. Explain client industry, problem, responsibilities, collaborators. Visuals carry the read.
- **Format, "How to write a design case study"** (useful). Be selective. Caption each image with copy. Open with client, date, and goal. Show setbacks and early drafts. Close with proof of success.
- **Wix, "How to write case studies"** (useful). One-line summary with client, date, role, teammates. State the brief's goal. Walk the stages in order, including abandoned ideas. Its sample page shows an unrendered template placeholder, so check output.
- **Opendoors Careers, "How to write a strong case study in 2025"** (useful). Put a measurable result in the title when one exists. Without a metric, name the outcome (clearer hierarchy, less friction). Organize around decisions and trade-offs. Its own title is instructional, a counter-example to its advice.
- **NN/g, "Writing Style for Print vs. Web"** (useful, for copy). Print is linear and author-driven. The web is reader-driven and needs actionable content. Front-load the first words of headings and links. Use fragments where they carry information. Cut words.
- **NN/g, "Quantifying UX Improvements: A Case Study"** (useful, strongest on metrics). One metric that reflects the experience, with a baseline. Reuse the same tasks afterwards. Report per-task results. Be candid about attribution.
- **NN/g, "Iterative Design and Prototype Testing of the Homepage"** (useful). Set design goals tied to outcomes and use them to settle trade-offs. Report where behavior contradicted what users said. Name constraints and admit what is unfinished.
- **NN/g, "UX Design Portfolios: Top Recommendations"** (useful). Three to five strong studies. Structure: problem, role, solution, impact, rejected ideas, learnings. Include process artifacts. Its 15% sales example is hypothetical; don't use it as a benchmark.
- **NN/g, "Portfolios for UX Researchers"** (useful). Organize around what hiring managers need. Show study plans and short quotes. Name your contribution. If under NDA, sanitize or get permission.
- **NN/g, "Making Cutting-Edge Technology Approachable: Facial-Recognition Payment"** (useful). Open with a real scenario. State sample size, participants, and methods. Organize findings as discrete problems, each with a fix and its trade-off. Compare against alternatives.
- **NN/g, "Writing Digital Copy for Domain Experts"** (useful, for copy). Lead with verified facts. Layer detail: summary first, depth below. Cite sources. Date time-sensitive content.
- **NN/g, "Iterative Design of a Survey Question"** (marginal to useful). Record each revision and the feedback that prompted it. Change one variable per round. Include failed versions.

## Skimmed

- **Johnyvino.com** (marginal). Not an article: a portfolio homepage. Project blurbs lead with a strong ownership verb and state the scope. The role is the first claim.
- **NN/g, "Pilot Testing"** (marginal). Only transfers as one line in a case study: how the method was validated.
- **NN/g, "Tesla's Big Touchscreen"** (marginal). Anchor the critique to one principle, annotate screenshots, walk through a failure scenario, include a "what works" section.
- **NN/g, "Doing UX in an Agile World"** (marginal). Describe the deadlines and staffing. Report skipped steps honestly. Cover stakeholder support or its absence.
- **NN/g, "Communicating Changes Throughout the Buyer's Journey: COVID-19"** (marginal). Open with the situation. Use numbered guidelines and annotated screenshots.

## Off-topic

- **NN/g, "Typography Terms: Glossary"** (off-topic for case study writing). Glossary page. Left-aligned body text and sparing bold aid scanning. Presentation note only.
